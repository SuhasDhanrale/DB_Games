GBL 2026 - Game Design Brief
Core idea:
We are not making five different games.
We are making five reasons to play every day.
1. Product Idea
A lightweight daily-games hub inside a news ecosystem, built for Indian non-gamers and casual players.
Each game gives the user a different reason to open the app and play for 1-3 minutes.
The brief asks for mass appeal, daily engagement, simple mechanics, long-term evolution and Indian audience relevance.
The audience is mobile-first, habit-driven and largely not active gamers. (Brief, pp. 9-11)

The key is choice. The same person may want excitement today, nostalgia tomorrow, a brain challenge
later, or a fresh daily discovery. The collection is the product.

4. Game Mechanics
Only the two unfinished games need to be designed further.
News Word
Core loop: Today's relevant news -> choose a 5-letter word -> player gets 5 attempts -> feedback
shows correct position / wrong position / absent letter -> reveal the connected story.
Mechanics: 5 letters, 5 guesses, green = correct position, yellow = correct letter/wrong position, grey =
absent. After the result, show the headline or a short explanation connected to the word.
Product difference: Do not position it as an Indian Wordle clone. Position it as Play today's news. The
news provides a fresh content source every day.
Odd One Out
Core loop: Show 4 familiar things -> player taps the one that does not belong -> reveal the relationship
-> explain the answer in one short line.
Content: food, places, festivals, sports, objects, language, culture, people and current events.
Design rule: There must always be one clearly defensible answer. The explanation is the reward: the
player should leave with a small piece of knowledge, not just a score.
Example
Vada Pav | Misal Pav | Poha | Litti Chokha
Player taps one -> Correct. Litti Chokha is associated with Bihar; the others are strongly associated with
Maharashtra.
5. Indian Relevance
Localise the content, not only the interface. Use regionally recognisable food, places, festivals, sports,
language and everyday objects. Then use the game to let players discover other parts of India.
Possible regional content pools: Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and other states. Avoid
turning this into stereotypes; the goal is recognition + curiosity.

6. Engagement + Progression
Keep the system light. The audience is not asking for a complicated live-service game.
Daily engagement
Every day: 5 games -> 5 daily challenges. The player can play one or all five. The content changes even
when the mechanics remain familiar.
Freshness sources: current news, regional content, festivals, cricket/events, seasons and other timely
topics.
Do not depend on coins, gems or reward spam. The brief explicitly says to avoid monetization loops and
reward-only gameplay. (Brief, p. 18)
Progression
Use mastery rather than a heavy meta-game.
GAME
SIMPLE PROGRESS SIGNAL
Gully Cricket
Personal best / accuracy / streak
Lagori
Accuracy / best score
News Word
Daily streak / solve rate
Odd One Out
Discovery score / streak
Dots & Boxes
Wins / best time / streak
Progression principle: The player gets better and discovers more. The system should reward mastery
and curiosity, not grinding.
7. Why Five Games?
A single game assumes one dominant player need. A daily collection allows the user to choose a mood:
fun, nostalgia, curiosity, discovery or thinking.
This is especially relevant for a mixed audience where gaming experience ranges from occasional to
none. The personas in the brief already describe familiarity with Ludo, Snakes and Ladders, carrom,
cards, matching and simple quizzes. (Brief, pp. 13-17)
Therefore the design target is not “hardcore gamer.” It is “I already understand how this feels, even if I
have never played this app.”

8. How We Sell the Idea
Five games. Five moods. One daily habit.
Core pitch
Dainik Bhaskar already gives India reasons to check in every day. We add reasons to play.
A daily collection of Indian games where every day gives you something new to play, discover and

9. Reference: Daily Games as a Product Pattern
The New York Times operates a single Games destination containing multiple recurring games such as
Wordle, Connections, Spelling Bee, Sudoku, Strands and crosswords. The useful design lesson for this
brief is not that “more games is always better”; it is that one destination can serve different play
preferences while maintaining a daily habit.
A separate study of casual game players found meaningful differences in the gratifications associated
with different genres, supporting the idea that players seek different experiences from games. Another
small study with adults 65+ found casual puzzle games were generally well accepted for enjoyment and
ease of play. These sources provide context, not proof that five games will outperform one.
10. Design Principle to Keep
Do not build five games and put them in one menu.
Build one daily product in which each game answers a
different human need.
This is the central argument for the jam presentation.
