# Design Rationale for Bhaskar Daily Games

How the brief and everyday habits led to five connected games

GBL 2026 submission | 12 October 2026

We propose one daily play destination within Dainik Bhaskar, with five complementary experiences. Each offers a small, satisfying interaction for a reader who may never think of themselves as a gamer. Our aim is to connect familiar play with language, reflection, sport, skill and understanding the news. This document explains how we arrived at the collection, why we chose these five games, and how the idea can evolve.

## Starting with the reader

The brief asks for mass appeal, simple mobile mechanics, Indian relevance and daily engagement that can evolve over time. Its core audience is Hindi-speaking, mobile-first readers aged 24–44 in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities; the personas also include an older reader. Their phone use fits around work, family and everyday news. We therefore designed for short breaks, clear Hindi instructions and familiar interactions. [1]

## How the idea developed

We began with recognisable games and daily puzzles. Reading the personas alongside our own everyday observations shifted the question to what would make a news reader want to play. The five needs below became our selection filter. In later reviews, we added meaning, relevant story previews and share cards, so that the result could offer something beyond a score. We also broadened single-topic packs into a mixed story collection, closer to the variety of a daily news feed.

## Why five games rather than one

One game gives us one primary entry point. A small collection lets readers choose a motivation and a mood. A reader might want cricket during a lunch break, a thoughtful card in the morning, or a word challenge later. Shared navigation, Hindi presentation and daily editions make the experiences feel related; completing one game is enough.

We kept experiences with a familiar starting point, a short clear finish and a distinct reason to return. These five balance two experiences built around language and thought, two familiar skill activities, and one factual discovery game, without creating a large catalogue. Five is our starting product hypothesis, not a number prescribed by the brief or proven to outperform one. Testing should decide which games deserve continued investment.

# Language and reflection

## Aaj Ka Shabd

आज का शब्द

**Why we chose it.** We were drawn to the daily word-puzzle format because the interaction stays understandable while a new answer provides a fresh challenge. It is an established product pattern: the publisher's NYT Games listing offers daily puzzles, including Wordle, and reports more than 10 million Android downloads for the overall app. That is evidence of the category's reach, rather than traction for our Hindi game. [2]

**Our connection to the reader.** We wanted this challenge to live in the language readers already use. Shabd uses five curated Hindi input units, six attempts and a compact keyboard of 24 reusable keys. Units can include matras or conjuncts; 24 describes the puzzle interface, not the Hindi alphabet. The limited input set is intended to make phone play approachable without requiring a full typing keyboard.

**Why it belongs in Bhaskar.** After the answer, its meaning and a specific story connection give the reader a reason to explore reporting. A language-technology story, for example, can make a word about multilingual access useful beyond the puzzle. The story remains optional. We use the familiar format as a starting point; Hindi input, editorial curation and the news journey are our contributions.

## Aaj Ki Niti

आज की नीति

**Why we chose it.** The personal spark was seeing an older uncle share a morning suvichar on WhatsApp. The meaningful action was sharing a thought with other people. Niti turns that observation into a small interaction: complete a thought, understand its meaning and make a card worth sending.

**Our connection to the reader.** We saw room for thoughts about relationships, judgment, patience and everyday social situations. A plausible choice invites reflection; the explanation gives the thought context. A wrong answer still reveals the meaning and allows sharing. The user's response should never become a public ranking of their values.

**Why it belongs in this collection.** Niti offers a calm, expressive experience alongside competitive skill play. A fresh thought is its daily return reason, and the card can continue the experience in a family or friends' conversation. The prototype uses original thoughts with clear attribution. Verified quotations and hosted invitations to play are future possibilities.

# Sport skill and discovery

## Aaj Ka Over

आज का ओवर | Super Over

**Why we chose it.** Cricket was our most immediate invitation to try a game. Our personal connection is enjoying its excitement with people of different ages; the brief's Rajesh persona follows cricket and plays gully cricket. A six-ball challenge gives a busy reader tension and a clear finish. [1]

**Its role in the collection.** Timing, shot choice and instant feedback offer physical skill and excitement alongside the quieter word and thought games. Readers who are drawn in by cricket can then discover another experience.

**Repeat reason and news fit.** New situations and improving execution can support repeated play. Sports reporting can supply context for a challenge. The prototype separates its authored practice targets and the player's simulated result from the actual match outcome, making both the play and its context clear.

## Aaj Ka Nishana

आज का निशाना | Carrom

**Why we chose it.** Carrom gives us an interaction that feels familiar before the reader learns the controls. A brief persona already plays carrom on family phones. We chose short layouts with limited shots, so readers can finish a challenge without committing to a full match. [1]

**Its role and repeat reason.** The satisfaction comes from judging an angle and improving a shot. New layouts can extend the same mechanic, while shots used make improvement understandable. Our social idea was the conversation outside the screen: "I cleared it in four shots; can you do it in three?" Optional friend challenges could support this later. Carrom is a useful evergreen break within a news app and does not need an artificial story connection.

## Aaj Ki Jodi

आज की जोड़ी | Jodo

**Why we chose it.** We wanted readers to recognise relationships in the world around them. Matching four pairs turns current events and useful general knowledge into a small discovery activity. The brief's quiz and matching preferences make it a relevant interaction to explore. [1]

**Its role and repeat reason.** Pairing a mission with its purpose, an athlete with an achievement, or a place with its state invites curiosity. The explanation is the reward, including for someone who did not know the answer. Mixed story packs give the game variety without making every edition a single-topic worksheet. Every pair needs a clear relationship and a checked source; related reading can deepen that discovery.

# One connected daily experience

The collection gives readers choice, while Bhaskar's editorial context gives that play a purpose. Shabd and Jodi naturally lead to explanations and reporting. Over can make a sports situation playable. Niti can offer a thought to share, with a related story when useful. Nishana provides a familiar skill break. Their contributions differ, so every result should offer the action that fits it.

## How the idea can grow

**First, strengthen the daily experience.** Make first play clear, preserve progress when readers open an article, and show useful personal results. Validate short sessions on ordinary phones. Simple mastery can mean fewer guesses, better timing or fewer shots; Niti can remain about reflection and sharing.

**Then expand through content and challenge design.** An editorial workflow could prepare checked daily packs, city editions, festival topics and verified sports scenarios. Gradually harder layouts and optional practice can add depth as readers improve. Long-term evolution needs both fresh content and meaningful challenge variation.

**Add social features where they earn a place.** Hosted share-to-play links could connect a Niti card recipient to that day's thought. Friend challenges could compare results under the same conditions, especially in Carrom or cricket. Archives and personal history are further possibilities. These are proposed extensions, not claims about current functionality.

## What we would test with readers

Track which game attracts a first start, which readers finish, and which brings them back on another day. Measure relevant story taps and engagement after play, then hosted share arrivals when available. Also check whether first-time players understand the controls and explanations, and how much editorial work each daily pack requires. Compare with the ordinary news journey before claiming an engagement benefit.

The value we propose for Bhaskar is a familiar, brief way to participate in its daily content. Its reporting can supply fresh questions and useful explanations; recognisable play gives readers an approachable way to engage with them. Reader testing should establish which of the five experiences makes the strongest contribution to that daily habit.
